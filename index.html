Abstract

Full-duplex voice interaction requires more than converting one complete utterance into another. A system must process speech as it arrives, decide when to take or yield the floor, and stop when the user interrupts, while preserving pretrained linguistic competence and acoustic paralinguistic cues. We present TASTE2, which transforms the original utterance-level TASTE method into an incremental dialogue stack.

A shared text-token vocabulary removes word-level averaging across the Speech Tokenizer, Spoken LM, and Speech Detokenizer. Modality-aligned dialogue training predicts one continuous audio latent for each text token without interleaving heterogeneous token streams, and an incremental Speech Detokenizer enables streaming synthesis through CosyVoice2. After speech and dialogue training, TASTE2 (Merge) reaches 56.3% on LLaMA-Questions against a 57.3% Qwen2.5-7B Instruct text-only reference, a 98.2% accuracy retention. We also build TASTE2 VoiceBot, a working research system that processes user speech incrementally, streams synthesized audio, and stops generation when the user barges in.

Finally, to our knowledge, we provide the first systematic characterization of explicit paralinguistic control in a TASTE-based model. Speaking-rate control serves as a cross-strategy proof of concept after dialogue SFT, while emotion control is strategy dependent and the remaining attributes stay weak.

Method

Shared vocabulary

A common text-token vocabulary removes word-level averaging and language-dependent segmentation.

Aligned prediction

The Spoken LM predicts one continuous audio latent per text token without lengthening its sequence.

Streaming synthesis

An incremental Speech Detokenizer produces S3 units and synthesizes audio through CosyVoice2.

Paralinguistic Control

Because one continuous audio latent travels with every text token, the Spoken LM can carry acoustic intent alongside its words. We test explicit, instruction-conditioned control on a 41-item subset of VStyle, rated 1–5 by three human raters. Speaking rate is the attribute that survives dialogue SFT under both training strategies; the samples below are two of its highest-rated generations.

Conclusion

TASTE2 establishes text-aligned speech modeling as a practical foundation for full-duplex voice interaction, and the evaluations support a specific claim rather than a general one. Pairing one continuous audio latent with each text token preserves 98.2% of the text reference's benchmark accuracy while emitting acoustic information at every step, and it delivers the full-duplex behavior that depends most directly on that representation: barge-in handling at 0.060 s and turn identification without a dedicated classifier. The deployed VoiceBot demonstrates the same stack under incremental processing and streaming synthesis. Natural-conversation robustness, speech-generation latency, and feature-general paralinguistic control remain open limits.
